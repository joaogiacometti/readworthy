Mayonnaise is an emulsion: tiny droplets of oil suspended in a small amount of water, from the lemon juice and the egg yolk. Oil and water don't mix on their own. Lecithin and proteins in the yolk coat each droplet, with one end attracted to water and the other to oil, and keep the droplets from merging.

That is why the oil has to go in slowly at first. The yolk can only coat so many new droplets at a time; pour too fast and the droplets merge, and the sauce breaks into a greasy puddle. Once a stable base has formed, you can add oil faster. To rescue a broken mayonnaise, start again with a fresh yolk, or a spoonful of water, and whisk the broken sauce into it drop by drop.
