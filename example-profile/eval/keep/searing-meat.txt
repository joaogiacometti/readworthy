What searing meat really does

Searing doesn't "seal in the juices"; a seared steak loses about as much moisture as an unseared one. What searing does is flavor. Above roughly 140°C, amino acids and sugars on the surface react in the Maillard reaction, producing hundreds of new aroma compounds and the brown crust we associate with roasted meat.

Two things stop it from happening. Water: a wet surface can't rise much above 100°C until the water has evaporated, so pat the meat dry, and salt it well ahead or right before cooking, not ten minutes before, when the salt has drawn moisture out but not yet let it be reabsorbed. Crowding: too many pieces in the pan drop its temperature and release steam, so the meat boils in its own liquid. Cook in batches, in a pan that was hot before the meat went in.
